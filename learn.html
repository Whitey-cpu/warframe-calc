How the math works

A walk through every step between a weapon's listed stats and the DPS number, in the order the calculator does it. Each section links the WARFRAME Wiki page it is based on. Anything the wiki does not state outright is marked as an assumption in the calculator's notes.

1. Base damage

Every attack lists damage split into types: Impact, Puncture and Slash (physical) and sometimes an element. Kuva, Tenet, Coda and Dual Coda weapons add a Valence bonus: 25 to 60% of base damage as one extra type (Impact, Heat, Cold, Electricity, Toxin, Magnetic or Radiation), counted as base damage. Elemental mods scale from that raised base. For combining, a Valence primary is treated as innate and joins after mod slots in HCET order (Heat → Cold → Electricity → Toxin). Valence Fusion raises the percent: min(1.1 × max(A, B), 60%), floored to 0.1%, then ≥58% snaps to 60%.

Sources: Damage, Kuva Weapons, Valence Fusion, Progenitor

2. Damage mods

Base damage mods (Serration, Point Blank, Primary Merciless stacks) add together into one pool, then multiply every damage type:

damage = base x (1 + sum of damage bonuses)

"Direct Damage" from Galvanized Aptitude, Savvy and Shot joins the same pool for hits. Faction mods (Bane, Cleanse, Expel) are a separate multiplier, applied later against that faction only.

Source: Damage calculation

3. Elements

An elemental mod adds its % of the modded base damage as a new type. Two primary elements next to each other (by slot order, top left first) combine:

CombinationResult
Heat + ColdBlast
Heat + ElectricityRadiation
Heat + ToxinGas
Cold + ElectricityMagnetic
Cold + ToxinViral
Electricity + ToxinCorrosive

A weapon's own innate primary elements and any Valence primary join after the mod slots, in HCET order (Heat, Cold, Electricity, Toxin). Matching a modded primary merges into that mod's type instead of opening a new slot.

Sources: Damage overview, Dual Coda Torxica (HCET table)

4. Quantization

The game rounds each damage type to a multiple of 1/32 of the attack's base damage. Small elemental bonuses can round away; that is why the arsenal screen and the actual hit can differ slightly.

type damage = round(type damage / (base / 32)) x (base / 32)

Source: Quantization

5. Armor and factions

Armor reduces damage taken from most types, up to 90% at 2,700 armor. Above 2,700 (exceptional cases) it becomes 300 / (armor + 300). Each damage type always gets at least 1 damage through armor:

damage taken = 1 - 0.9 x sqrt(armor / 2700) (armor up to 2700)

Corrosive stacks and Heat strip armor. Each faction is weak (x1.5) or resistant (x0.5) to some types. Viral stacks raise damage to health: +100% at the first stack, +25% per extra stack, up to +325%.

Sources: Armor, Status Effect

6. Critical hits

Crit chance = base x (1 + crit mods). Above 100%, every hit crits and the remainder is the chance of a higher tier (orange, then red):

crit multiplier at tier n = n x (crit damage - 1) + 1

The calculator averages this over all hits. On heads with the critical headshot flag (most Grineer, Infested and Sentient; not Corpus humanoids, most of The Murmur or Techrot), a headshot crit doubles the weapon's base crit multiplier, and crit damage mods still apply on top. The weak point multiplier applies to headshots on top of that.

Sources: Critical Hit, Weak Point

7. Status effects

Each pellet rolls status chance on its own. The status type is picked in proportion to each damage type's share. Slash, Heat, Toxin, Gas and Electricity deal damage over time; Viral, Corrosive, Magnetic, Puncture and Cold change how much damage the target takes.

Heat is the exception: every Heat proc adds a stack and resets the timer of all Heat stacks, so they keep ticking while procs keep coming. Each proc's ticks are therefore counted over how long the shared timer stays alive (the calculator works this out in closed form and the shot-by-shot simulation checks it).

Assumption: expected procs per shot = status chance x multishot. Statuses you apply yourself build up over the fight when "My own status effects" is on.

Sources: Status Effect, Damage over Time

8. Fire rate, reload and sustained DPS

Burst DPS is damage per shot times shots per second. Sustained DPS also counts the time spent reloading:

sustained = burst x magazine / (magazine + fire rate x reload time)

Battery weapons (Coda Bubonico, Fulmin, Cycron) refill on their own. Reload speed only shortens the delay before the refill starts:

refill time = delay / (1 + reload speed) + magazine / refill rate

Sources: Reload, Battery Weapon

9. On Kill and stacking buffs

Buffs like Galvanized Hell, Primary Merciless and Primary Frostbite build stacks. How many you actually hold depends on how often you trigger them and how they wear off:

BuffWhen the timer runs out
Galvanized mods, Merciless, DeadheadOne stack is lost and the timer restarts
Galvanized Scope, Galvanized CrosshairsEach stack has its own timer
Primary Frostbite, Blight, Crux, Conjunction VoltageAll stacks are lost at once

The calculator turns your kill rate (or your proc and headshot rate for on-proc arcanes) into the average number of stacks over a long fight. Galvanized mods (20s per stack, one lost at a time) stay near full even at one kill every few seconds; Primary Merciless (4s) needs a steady stream of kills.

Assumption: kills and procs arrive at a steady random rate, and each kill restarts the timer.

Sources: Galvanized Mods, Primary Merciless, Primary Frostbite

10. Damage falloff and range

Many shotguns and some other weapons lose damage with distance: full damage up to a start distance, then a straight drop to a minimum at the end distance.

damage x (1 - max reduction x clamp((distance - start) / (end - start), 0, 1))

Example from the wiki: Hek keeps 100% up to 10m and drops to 20% at 20m. Area attacks lose damage away from the blast centre in the same way. Buffs with a range condition (Shotgun Vendetta: kill within 5m) follow the target distance you set.

Assumption: status damage over time falls off the same way as the hit.

Source: Damage Falloff

11. Melee: stances, combo and heavy attacks

Stances. Each combo hit deals a % of the weapon's damage, and some hits carry extra Slash or forced procs. Attack speed shortens the whole combo:

hits per second = hits in combo / (combo length at speed 1.0 / attack speed)

Melee Combo. The counter gives 2x at 20 hits and +1x every 20 hits up to 12x at 220. It does not multiply normal swings; it multiplies heavy attacks and powers Blood Rush (crit) and Weeping Wounds (status).

Heavy attacks. The listed heavy damage is normal base damage times the weapon class heavy multiplier. A heavy attack spends the whole combo unless Heavy Attack Efficiency keeps some (capped at 90%). Wind-up is shortened only by Heavy Attack Wind Up Speed, not attack speed.

heavy hit = base x (1 + damage mods) x (1 + elements) x heavy multiplier x combo multiplier

Assumption: one heavy attack takes its wind-up plus one normal swing.

Sources: Melee, Melee Combo, Stance, stance data

12. Archon Shards

Five slots per Warframe. The weapon bonuses add to the matching mods; Tauforged shards are 1.5x:

ShardWeapon bonus
Crimson+25% melee crit damage, +25% primary status chance or +25% secondary crit chance
Violet+30% primary Electricity, +10% per Crimson, Azure or Violet shard; or +25% melee crit damage (doubled over 500 max energy)
Topaz+1% secondary crit chance per kill on an enemy with Heat, up to 50%
Emerald+30% Toxin status damage, or +2 max Corrosive stacks

Assumption: shard Electricity joins the element order after your mods.

Sources: Archon Shard, Violet, Topaz, Emerald

13. The Simulacrum

The Simulacrum tab (Advanced mode) holds every switch above: turn a mechanic off to see what it is worth, set the kill rate and distance, and watch the first minute of a fight as stacks and statuses build up. The rest of the site, including the optimizer and Teshin, uses the same settings.

14. Riven mods and crafting odds

Every riven trait follows one formula from the wiki: value = base × 10 × (rank + 1) × 1.2 × L × W. The base depends on the trait and the weapon class. L depends on the layout: 0.99 with 2 positives, 1.2375 with 2 positives and a negative (the negative gets −0.495), 0.75 with 3 positives, and 0.9375 with 3 positives and a negative (−0.75). W is the roll, from 0.9 to 1.1.

The 1.2 used to be each weapon's own Riven Disposition, anywhere from 0.5 to 1.55. Hotfix 44.1.3 (October 22, 2026) removed dispositions, so every riven now rolls as if it had 1.2. A riven value from before that date converts as old value × 1.2 / old disposition. The Riven tab keeps DE's final list so it can do this for you.

Cycling costs 900 Kuva the first time, rising to 3,500 from the 10th cycle on. Locking a trait keeps it and the layout, but doubles the cost. A Riven Splicer turns two listed traits into one spliced trait. The spliced trait is always positive, keeps the higher roll of the two, and does not double the cost.

The crafting odds count every way a cycle can land and add up the ones that meet your goal. DE has not published the cycling odds, so two inputs come from community testing: each layout comes up about 1 time in 4, and every trait the weapon can roll is equally likely. You can change the layout odds on the Riven tab. The splicing planner plays the whole plan hundreds of times instead (cycle, lock, splice, keep the better version), because the order of those choices matters.

Assumptions: rolls are spread evenly between the lowest and highest value, and a physical trait (Impact, Puncture, Slash) is only in the pool when it makes up more than 25% of the hit. The wiki says "usually", so you can switch those traits.

Sources: Riven Mods, Hotfix 44.1.3, Update 44 (Trait Locking), community odds thread
